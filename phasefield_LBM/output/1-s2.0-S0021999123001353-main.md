# a   b   s   t   r   a   c   t

In   this   paper,   we   proposed   a   central   moment   discrete   uniﬁed   gas-kinetic   scheme   (DUGKS)   for   multiphase   ﬂows   with   large   density   ratio   and   high   Reynolds   number.   Two   sets   of   kinetic   equations   with   central-moment-based   multiple   relaxation   time   collision   operator   are   employed   to   approximate   the   incompressible   Navier-Stokes   equations   and   a   conservative   phase   ﬁeld   equation   for   interface-capturing,   respectively.   In   the   framework   of   DUGKS,   the   ﬁrst   moment   of   the   distribution   function   for   the   hydrodynamic   equations   is   deﬁned   as   velocity   instead   of   momentum.   Meanwhile,   the   zeroth   moments   of   the   distribution   function   and   external   force   are   carefully   deﬁned   such   that   a   artiﬁcial   pressure   evolution   equation   can   be   recovered.   Moreover,   the   Strang   splitting   technique   for   time   integration   is   employed   to   avoid   the   calculation   of   spatial   derivatives   in   the   force   term   at   cell   faces.   For   the   interface-capturing   equation,   two   equivalent   DUGKS   methods   that   deal   with   the   diffusion   term   differently   using   a   source   term   as   well   as   a   modiﬁed   moments   of   the   equilibrium   distribution   function   are   presented.   Several   benchmark   tests   that   cover   a   wide   a   range   of   density   ratios   (up   to   1000)   and   Reynolds   numbers   (up   to   10 5 )   are   subsequently   carried   out   to   demonstrate   the   capabilities   of   the   proposed   scheme.   Numerical   results   are   in   good   agreement   with   the   theoretical   and   experimental   data.

# 1.   Introduction

Numerical   simulation   of   multiphase   ﬂows   is   of   interest   in   a   variety   of   natural   and   engineering   processes.   Many   numerical   methods,   such   as   the   front   tracking   method [ 1 ],   volume   of   ﬂuid [ 2 ],   level   set   method [ 3 ],   diffuse   interface   method [ 4 , 5 ] and   kinetic-theory-based   method [ 6 ],   have   been   proposed.   In   particular,   many   kinetic-theory-based   methods,   such   as   lattice   Boltzmann   equation   (LBE)   method   [ 7 , 8 ],   gas-kinetic   scheme [ 9 , 10 ],   multiphase   lattice   Boltzmann   ﬂux   solver [ 11 –

13 ],   discrete   uniﬁed   gas   kinetic   scheme   method [ 14 – 16 ],   have   been   developed   into   alternative   and   promising   numerical   schemes   to   simulate   complex   multiphase   ﬂows.   The   most   popular   kinetic   method   is   the   LBE   method   due   to   its   locality   of   the   streaming   and   collision   algorithm   and   ease   of   implementation.   Based   on   the   used   strategies   of   dealing   with   the   intermolecular   interactions   for   non-ideal   gas   effects,   various   multiphase   LBE   methods   have   been   developed.   To   date,   a   variety   of   successful   applications   for   multiphase   ﬂows   have   been   achieved   using   these   LBE   methods.   Recent   and   comprehensive   review   of   LBE   models   for   multiphase   ﬂows   can   be   found   in   several   papers [ 17 , 18 ].                                    

However, most of the previously proposed LBE methods are restricted to two-phase ﬂuids with small or moderate density differences   (less   than   100)   due   to   numerical   instability [ 19 – 22 ].   Although   some   improved   methods   have   been   developed,   numerical   modeling   of   multiphase   ﬂows   with   complex   interface   morphology   at   large   density   contrast   still   remains   a   challenging   subject   in   multiphase   community.   In   the   framework   of   LBE,   the   adopted   strategies   to   improve   the   numerical   stability   could   be   roughly   divided   into   four   categories.   The   ﬁrst   strategy   is   to   improve   the   free-divergence   condition.   For   example,   Inamuro   et   al. [ 23 ] employed   one   particle   velocity   distribution   function   for   the   calculation   of   a   predicted   velocity   of   the   two-phase   ﬂuid   without   the   pressure   gradient.   Then,   the   current   velocity   is   determined   by   iteratively   solving   the   pressure   Poisson   equation   at   each   time   step.   The   second   strategy   is   to   adopt   special   discretization   scheme.   For   example,   Lee   et   al. [ 24 ] proposed   a   mixing   difference   scheme   for   the   surface   tension   force   in   the   pre-streaming   collision   step   while   using   the   standard   central   difference   scheme   for   the   surface   tension   force   in   the   post-streaming,   which   is   able   to   improve   the   numerical   stability.   However,   it   was   proved   that   such   treatment   violates   the   conservation   of   mass [ 25 ].   Similarly,   Gong   and   Cheng [ 26 ] proposed   a   mixed   interparticle   interaction   force   term   in   the   single-component   multiphase   pseudo-potential   model   to   improve   the   numerical   stability.   The   third   strategy   is   to   couple   Navier-Stokes   (NS)   equations   with   an   improved   interface-tracking   equation,   such   as   the   level   set   equation,   the   volume   of   ﬂuid   equation   and   the   corrected   phase   ﬁeld   equation.   In   particular,   the   conservative   Allen-Cahn   equation   (ACE)   that   is   a   second   order   partial   differential   equation   has   attracted   much   attention [ 27 ].   By   employing   the   ACE   for   interface-capturing,   several   LBE   models   for   multiphase   ﬂow   with   large   density   ratios   have   been   proposed [ 28 – 30 ].   In   these   models,   the   LBE   methods   for   the   ACE   are   almost   similar.   But   the   deﬁnitions   of   the   equilibrium   distribution   function   and   source   term   for   the   hydrodynamic   equations   are   different.   Moreover,   some   modiﬁed   Cahn-Hilliard   equations   with   interface   correction   terms   are   also   presented   to   track   the   interface   with   improved   accuracy   such   that   LBE   is   able   to   simulate   two   phase   ﬂows   with   large   density   ratios [ 31 , 32 ].   The   fourth   strategy   is   to   employ   improved   collision   operators,   such   as   multiple   entropic   collision   model [ 33 , 34 ],   central   moment-based   collision   model [ 35 ] and   cumulant   collision   model [ 36 ] instead   of   the   classical   single-relaxation   time   and   multiple-relaxation   -time   models.   For   example,   Gruszczynski   et   al. [ 37 ] and   Hajabdollahi   et   al. [ 38 ] separately   presented   central-moment-based   lattice   Boltzmann   model   for   the   incompressible   two-phase   ﬂows.   Hosseini   and   Karlin   et   al. [ 39 ] proposed   a   multiple   relaxation   time   entropic   LBE   model.   Sitompul   and   Aoki   et   al. [ 40 ] presented   a   ﬁltered   cumulant   LBE   method   to   simulate   violent   twophase   ﬂow   problems.   It’s   worth   pointing   out   that   the   above   strategies   are   not   independent   of   each   other.   In   most   models,   several   strategies   are   usually   adopted   together.   In   addition,   these   models   were   claimed   to   be   able   to   simulate   two-phase   ﬂow   problems   with   large   density   ratio   in   which   both   the   Reynolds   number   and   interface   deformation   are   relatively   small.

On   the   other   hand,   the   popular   LBE   method   suffers   from   substantial   drawbacks,   such   as   the   uniform   grid   and   ﬁxed   CFL   condition.   Recently,   Guo   developed   a   discrete   uniﬁed   gas   kinetic   scheme   for   multiscale   ﬂows   based   on   kinetic   model [ 14 , 16 ].   A   feature   of   the   DUGKS   is   that   a   numerical   characteristic   solution   of   the   kinetic   equation   is   employed   to   reconstruct   the   cell-interface   ﬂux.   With   the   consideration   of   both   free   streaming   and   collision   physics   in   the   ﬂux   reconstruction,   it   has   been   shown   that   the   DUGKS   is   able   to   simulate   different   regime   ﬂow   behavior   form   the   continuum   regime   to   the   freemolecular   regime.   Even   in   the   continuum   regime,   the   ﬁnite-volume   formulation   and   the   release   of   tight   coupling   between   time   step   and   mesh   size   make   DUGKS   a   competitive   tool,   such   as   easy   to   employ   arbitrary   unstructured   meshes   and   fully   adjustable   CFL.   Wang   et   al. [ 41 ] made   a   comparison   between   DUGKS   and   standard   LBE   and   Zhu   et   al. [ 42 ] made   a   comparison   between   DUGKS   and   the   so-called   off-lattice   Boltzmann   method,   which   numerically   demonstrated   the   superior   accuracy   and   stability   of   the   DUGKS   for   high   Reynolds   ﬂows.   Considering   these   features,   Zhang   et   al. [ 43 ] ﬁrst   extended   the   DUGKS   to   two-phase   ﬂows   based   on   a   quasi-incompressible   NS   equations   coupled   with   the   Cahn-Hilliard   equation   for   interface   capturing.   Yang   et   al. [ 44 ] further   extended   the   DUGKS   to   two   ﬂuids   with   large   density   ratios   by   solving   the   incompressible   NS   equations   coupled   with   the   conservative   ACE.   However,   the   accuracy   of   capturing   interface   is   not   satisfactory   and   the   ability   of   their   model   for   multiphase   ﬂow   with   complex   interface   deformation   is   unclear.   More   recently,   Zhang [ 45 ] proved   that   a   linear   reconstruction   for   the   distribution   function   at   cell   face   in   DUGKS   fails   to   recover   the   kinetic   equation   with   second-order   accuracy   when   the   source   term   is   involved   or   the   collision   operator   fails   to   satisfy   the   momentum   conservation.   They   instead   proposed   a   piecewise-parabolic   reconstruction   for   the   distribution   function   at   cell   face.   In   particular,   the   weighted   essentially   non-oscillatory   scheme   for   the   reconstruction   of   the   distribution   function   at   the   cell   face   can   signiﬁcantly   improve   the   accuracy   of   interface   capturing.

In this paper, we proposed a robust and accurate DUGKS for two-phase fl ows with large density ratios and high Reynolds number. The features of the present model are summarized as follows. First, the central moment-based collision operator is utilized to improve the Galilean invariance, in which the central moment is derived from the continuous Maxwell equilibrium distribution function instead of a discrete one. Second, for the hydrodynamic equations, a velocity-based particle distribution function with a constant density is adopted such that a differentiation-by-part error proportional to the density ratio in the recovered equations is removed [46]. In particular, the pressure only appears in the zeroth moment and no pressure appears in the higher order moment, which is significantly different from the treatment in the previous LBE models [20-22,43,28]. To update the evolution of pressure, an artificial compressibility equation that can be recovered by re- defining the external force in the DUGKS is used to update the pressure. Third, the Strang splitting method is applied to the DUGKS with a force term that is used to recover the hydrodynamic equations. This is able to avoid calculations of various spatial derivatives at cell interfaces, which could contribute to improving numerical stability. Finally, to match the conservative phase fi eld equation, both central moment DUGKS methods for the conservative ACE are presented. The moments of the equilibrium distribution are obtained from the continuous Maxwell equilibrium distribution function. Compared with the previous models [37,38], we directly modified the central moments instead of redefining the original distribution function. Through the multiscale expansion analysis, we proved that the correct macroscopic equations can be recovered from both sets of kinetic equations.

# 4.   Results   and   discussions

In   this   section,   we   will   present   a   validation   study   of   our   proposed   DUGKS   by   a   series   of   benchmark   problems.   First,   the   ﬁrst   three   tests   are   used   to   evaluate   the   accuracy   of   capturing   the   interface   and   modeling   the   surface   tension   effect.   The   last   four   tests   are   used   to   validate   the   accuracy   and   robustness   of   the   present   model   for   simulating   large-density-ratio   ﬂows   with   complex   interface   topologies.   For   each   test,   in   the   presence   of   walls,   the   missing   distribution   functions   are   obtained   by   the   bounce-back   scheme [ 62 ],   which   means   that   the   velocities   and   normal   gradient   of   the   order   parameter   remain   zero   at   the   boundary.   For   the   free-slip   boundary   condition,   the   missing   distribution   functions   for   the   hydrodynamic   equations   are   realized   by   specular   reﬂection   scheme,   which   means   that   the   normal   velocity   remains   zero.   For   the   pressure   boundary,   the   zero   normal   pressure   gradient   at   the   boundary   is   enforced.   The   open   boundary   is   realized   by   setting   zero   normal   gradients   of   distribution   function,   order   parameter   and   pressure. # 5.   Summary

In   this   paper   a   central   moment-based   discrete   uniﬁed   gas-kinetic   scheme   has   been   developed   for   the   accurate   and   robust   modeling   of   incompressible   two-phase   ﬂows   with   large   density   ratios   and   high   Reynolds   numbers.   In   terms   of   the   hydrodynamics   equations,   the   velocity-based   distribution   function   is   employed   to   approximate   the   formulations   of   nonconserved   momentum   equation   associated   with   an   pressure   evolution   equation.   The   central   moment   collision   operator   is   adopted   to   improve   the   Galilean   invariance,   where   the   continuous   form   of   the   Maxwell-Boltzmann   equilibrium   distribution   function   is   used   to   calculate   the   central   moments   for   the   hydrodynamic   ﬁelds.   To   the   best   of   our   knowledge,   this   is   the   ﬁrst   time   that   central   moments   collision   operator   is   applied   to   DUGKS.   To   further   enhance   the   numerical   stability,   the   Strange-splitting   scheme   in   time   is   employed   so   that   the   calculations   of   complex   source   terms   at   cell   faces   is   missing.   In   terms   of   interfacecapturing,   two   DUGKS   methods   with   and   without   force   term   for   the   conservative   ACE   are   presented.   It   is   noted   that   the   moments   of   the   distribution   function   are   directly   modiﬁed   to   match   the   correct   ACE   that   is   different   from   those   in   the   previous   LBE   and   DUGKS   methods   for   the   ACE.

To   validate   the   performance   of   the   present   model,   the   accuracy   of   both   DUGKS   methods   for   interface   capturing   is   ﬁrst   validated   through   simulating   the   reversed   single   vortex.   The   results   from   both   DUGKS   for   ACE   are   nearly   identical.   Due   to   computational   cost,   the   DUGKS   without   force   term   for   ACE   is   used   in   simulations.   Then,   the   ability   to   model   the   surface   tension   effect   of   the   present   solver   is   demonstrated   by   two   other   benchmarks   problems,   that   is,   static   droplet   and   Oscillating   drop.   Finally,   the   present   solver   is   applied   to   simulate   Rayleigh-Taylor   instability,   rising   bubble,   droplet   splashing   and   dam   break   ﬂow   over   dry   and   wet   beds.   These   problems   cover   a   wide   a   range   of   density   ratios,   Reynolds   numbers   and   Weber   numbers.   And   the   complex   topological   changes   of   two-phase   interfaces   from   deformation   to   breakup   have   been   examined   and   compared.   The   results   are   in   excellent   agreement   with   the   previous   computational   and   experimental   results   qualitatively   and   quantitatively.

For   the   present   method,   the   derivatives   of   the   density   and   pressure   can   be   evaluated   by   some   advanced   techniques   such   as   implicit   high-order   compact   difference   or   high-order   total   variation   diminishing   scheme,   then   the   numerical   stability   of   the   present   DUGKS   can   be   further   enhanced.   In   particular,   it   is   possible   to   eliminate   spurious   velocities   by   carefully   handling   discretization   of   both   the   pressure   gradient   and   the   surface   tension   force.   In   addition,   several   artiﬁcial   pressure   evolution   equations   modiﬁed   with   a   convective   term   or   a   diffusive   term   can   be   coupled   into   the   present   model   to   produce   a   more   accurate   pressure   ﬁeld [ 79 ].   The   strategy   adopted   in   the   present   DUGKS   can   also   be   employed   to   improve   the   phase-ﬁeldbased   two-phase   LBE   models.   It   is   also   noted   that   the   central-moment-based   DUGKS   with   nine   discrete   velocities   in   present   work   fails   to   eliminate   all   the   third   order   terms   of   velocity   although   improvement   has   been   made.   This   means   that   the   property   of   Galilean   invariance   is   not   completely   guaranteed.   An   available   method   to   address   this   is   to   carefully   correct   the   raw   moments   of   the   source   term.   In   addition,   an   extension   of   the   present   method   to   three-dimensions   with   more   discrete   velocities   for   simulation   of   mutliphase   ﬂows   may   also   address   this   deﬁciency.   To   sum   up,   the   present   DUGKS   method   could   be   a   promising   tool   to   expand   applicability   of   multiphase   ﬂows   in   real-life   conditions.
